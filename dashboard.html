02

Five things worth acting on

01

Everyone claims a moat, and no two are made of the same material

Autodesk owns the supply-side bid graph, Procore the project record, Causeway UK transaction flow, Kojo live materials spend, Felix vendor compliance history. ProcurePro owns something none of them have at line-item depth: 200,000 awarded trade packages with priced scope and exclusions attached.

02

The category moved from reading to acting, and we are one rung back

Procore ships an agent library with Digital Coworker packaging, and Kojo’s agents chase distributors for delivery dates. BidLevel extracts, structures and compares in three minutes, which is strong document AI. A US buyer will ask what it does, not what it reads.

03

The competitor easiest to miss in the UK presents as finance software

Causeway is pushing AI upstream from invoicing into “automated trade mark-up and supplier selection”, with a 59,000-supplier network already transacting. That description overlaps our own work, and it comes from a 20-year incumbent in the market we are scaling into now.

04

Autodesk’s network is real in America and close to worthless elsewhere

BuildingConnected’s crowdsourced density is a US phenomenon. In Australia and the UK it approximates a cold start, which is why we won at home. The US entry needs a route around that density, and there is one.

05

Nobody credible is serving the award decision with agentic depth

Procore’s agents point at RFIs, submittals and contract review. Autodesk’s AI recommends bidders and reads subcontractor financials. Kojo serves a different buyer. That gap is currently unattended, and it is exactly where our data sits.

Autodesk

Direct BuildingConnected and TradeTapp. The largest supply-side network in US commercial construction, and the main obstacle to a US entry. 350,000+ projectsUS$3.6k–5k+/yr

AI data moat

The strongest supply-side network in the category and the hardest to replicate. BuildingConnected operates the largest crowdsourced network in commercial construction, used to vet subcontractors and compare financial health.1 The Recommendation Engine suggests bidders by location, trade expertise and past performance, and TradeTapp turns PDF financial statements into structured data.2 The corpus is bid-graph shaped: who was invited, who bid, who won. What it lacks at our granularity is the priced line-item scope of the awarded package with exclusions attached. Their data is about who, ours is about what and for how much.

Automated workflows

Autodesk AI spans BuildingConnected Pro, Bid Board Pro and TradeTapp. A Bidding tool Beta in Autodesk Forma brings bid management and prequalification into the Forma preconstruction experience.3 The automation is recommendation and extraction rather than agentic execution.

Product overlap

Strong on tenders, vendor management and prequalification. Partial on compare and recommend, at bid level rather than priced scope level. Absent on procurement schedule, scope of works library and contracts as a commercial workflow. The overlap is real on the front half of the funnel and thin on the back half.

Their case against us

  • The network. A US GC can find and qualify subcontractors they have never worked with.
  • Subcontractor financial risk data via TradeTapp, which we do not have.
  • Single-vendor consolidation for firms already standardised on Autodesk.

Risks for ProcurePro

  1. Highest likelihood. US entry meets a dense network we cannot match, and buying decisions default to BuildingConnected.
  2. Forma Bidding Beta deepens down-funnel into priced-scope comparison.
  3. Autodesk owns Payapps, so it can bundle procurement to payment.

Opportunities

  • Their data answers who should bid. Ours answers whether the price and scope are right.
  • They are slow on agentic workflow. BidLevel already does something they have not shipped.
  • Outside North America the network advantage largely evaporates.

Open questions

  • Does the Forma Bidding Beta roadmap include priced line-item comparison and scope-gap detection?
  • Is Autodesk training models on the bid graph, or only using it for retrieval?

Procore

Direct (bundler) The platform incumbent in every market we are entering, and an integration partner. It does not need to be better at procurement, only adequate and already installed. 16,000+ companies~US$300m R&D

AI data moat

Breadth of project system-of-record data, plus bought capability to act on it. Procore acquired Datagrid on 20 January 2026, bringing agents for deep search, RFI validation, scope checks, prequalification, audits and daily reports. Datagrid’s founder now leads Procore’s AI and data strategy.4 The moat is horizontal reach rather than depth: Procore sees the whole project but not the commercial detail of how a trade package was priced and awarded, because that decision usually happens before or outside Procore.

Automated workflows

The most aggressive agentic positioning in the category. On 23 July 2026 Procore announced Digital Coworker packages, an expanded AI Agent Library, and previewed Procore Skills, which lets organisations teach Procore AI their own processes and standards.5 Skills is the strategically dangerous one. Shipped agent coverage is still concentrated on RFIs, submittals and contract review, not bid levelling.

Product overlap

Nothing at our depth. Partial on tenders, contracts, vendor management and purchase orders, all as modules inside a wider platform. The overlap is commercial rather than functional: Procore rarely wins on procurement capability, it wins when a customer decides one platform is worth more than the best tool.

Their case against us

  • One platform, one contract, one vendor relationship.
  • The broadest agent library shipping today.
  • Already installed at most of our target buyers.

Risks for ProcurePro

  1. Highest likelihood. The bundling squeeze. Close enough makes a second contract hard to justify.
  2. Procore Skills lets customers self-serve the workflow depth we currently sell.
  3. Partner-to-competitor drift. The integration also gives Procore a clear view of where the procurement gap sits in its own platform.

Opportunities

  • Their agents point at RFIs and submittals, not the award decision. That gap is unattended by the largest player.
  • “Keep Procore, add the procurement depth it does not have” beats a rip and replace.
  • Procurement will always compete internally for roadmap at Procore. Our whole roadmap is procurement.

Open questions

  • Is a procurement or bid-levelling agent on the announced roadmap? Nothing found publicly.
  • What happens to the integration if Procore ships competing depth?

Felix Group

Direct · Australia ASX-listed, Brisbane, same city and same buyers. Deeper on vendor compliance, earlier on AI, and combining with Nexvia for FY27. A$13.0m ARR, +51%115,000 vendors

AI data moat

A large vendor compliance and performance record, not yet turned into a shipped AI advantage. Felix positions its edge as connecting supply chain understanding to intelligent tendering.6 Its AI announcement makes no specific claim about proprietary datasets and describes discovery, experiments and testing with customers, with no release dates.7 The corpus is compliance-shaped: it knows whether a vendor is qualified, insured and performing, with much less of the priced commercial detail we hold.

Automated workflows

Stated targets are vendor assessment and bid analysis. An AI-driven Bid Insights feature is flagged for broader rollout, with vendor monetisation reporting changes from Q1 FY27.8 We are further along on shipped procurement AI, though public announcements are an imperfect proxy for what is running with customers.

Product overlap

Stronger than us on vendor management, prequalification and compliance. Partial on tenders and comparison. Absent at depth on procurement schedule, scope of works library and priced breakdown comparison. Nexvia is the piece to watch: a contractor-facing platform being integrated in the background with a combined go-to-market planned for FY27.

Their case against us

  • Deeper vendor compliance, ESG and risk tooling.
  • Reach beyond construction into mining, utilities and government.
  • Listed and audited, which some procurement functions prefer.

Risks for ProcurePro

  1. Moderate likelihood, real consequence. Felix plus Nexvia combined for FY27 is the closest anyone in Australia comes to our shape.
  2. Vendor-side monetisation could fund a supply network here that we do not have.
  3. Incumbency in government and infrastructure blocks a segment we might expand into.

Opportunities

  • Their AI work is still largely discovery by their own description. There is a window before the FY27 go-to-market.
  • Spread across six industries means less depth in commercial building specifically.
  • Our priced dataset answers their compliance data on a different axis, and it is the one that touches margin.

Open questions

  • What does Bid Insights do, and how does it compare to BidLevel on accuracy and speed?
  • What is the combined Felix and Nexvia product shape for FY27?
  • How much of the A$13m ARR comes from commercial head contractors?

Causeway Technologies

Direct · UK Entrenched on UK supply chain and finance rails, now moving upstream into preconstruction and supplier selection. Easy to miss because it presents as finance software. 59,000 suppliers300+ staff

AI data moat

Transaction flow across the UK construction supply chain, now being pushed upstream into decisions. CausewayOne is positioned as an AI-first platform with a construction-native intelligence model spanning projects, suppliers and finance.9 Tradex spans 59,000 suppliers and 750 customers.10 The corpus is invoice-shaped, which gives something close to a price index for UK construction supply. That is adjacent to our AI Pricing Library thesis, reasoning forward from settled transactions where we reason forward from awarded packages.

Automated workflows

AI is embedded in the preconstruction workflow with claimed efficiency gains of up to 75%, spanning estimate creation, BoQ ingestion, automated trade mark-up and supplier selection.11 That last phrase describes work we also do. The 75% figure appears on a campaign page rather than in product documentation, and no supporting customer case study was found, so it is best held as a claim to verify.

Product overlap

Strong on vendor management, supplier onboarding and supply chain transactions. Partial and growing on tenders, supplier selection and estimate-to-package handover. Absent on procurement schedule at our depth, scope of works library and priced trade comparison with exclusion extraction.

Their case against us

  • The supplier network already exists in the UK, transacting.
  • Procure-to-pay in one place, which finance directors like.
  • Twenty years of UK contractor relationships.

Risks for ProcurePro

  1. High likelihood. UK deals get reframed as procure-to-pay, where they argue the whole cycle and we argue one stage of it.
  2. Pricing intelligence from real invoices undercuts our AI Pricing Library pitch in the UK.
  3. Roll-up acquisitions could add preconstruction depth faster than building it.

Opportunities

  • Their centre of gravity is finance and settled transactions. They are not yet the tool a procurement manager opens to run a tender.
  • The efficiency claim has no public case study behind it. A documented side-by-side favours whoever has shipped, specific capability.
  • They are UK only. We can carry one story across ANZ, UK, Middle East and North America.

Open questions

  • What does “automated trade mark-up and supplier selection” do in the shipped product?
  • Is the 75% figure backed by a customer case study?
  • How much of the 59,000-supplier network is trade packages rather than materials?

Kojo

Adjacent · US Different buyer, same bet. The clearest working example of a procurement data moat built from transaction flow, and a read on what US buyers now expect. US$5bn/yr materials~US$94m raised

Why an adjacent player is in a top-five list

Kojo buys materials for specialty subcontractors, where we buy subcontractors for head contractors. It is here because it sets the expectation US buyers bring to any procurement AI pitch, not because we will meet it in deals.

AI data moat

Live transaction flow plus a distributor’s rails. The Kojo Intelligence Layer uses GPT-4 and transformer models alongside statistical methods to find project-appropriate materials at optimal prices.12 More than 600 contractors purchase over US$5bn of materials annually through the platform.13 Taking investment from distributor Wesco converts a data moat into a supply relationship, which a software-only competitor cannot replicate by shipping features.14

Automated workflows

Agents assist with procurement activities, scheduling and distributor follow-ups, and the Wesco integration automates submittals, ordering and delivery scheduling. Kojo has crossed from “AI reads your documents” into “AI completes the transaction”. Chasing a distributor for a delivery date is a task, not a summary.

Their case against us

  • A real-time price signal from actual purchases.
  • Agents that execute, including third-party follow-up.
  • A supply chain partner with a balance sheet behind the product.

Risks for ProcurePro

  1. Highest likelihood. Expectation setting. US buyers will ask what our agents do, not what our AI reads.
  2. Materials and subcontract procurement converge in the head contractor’s total spend picture.
  3. Distributor-backed economics are a pricing model we cannot currently match.

Opportunities

  • Nobody with Kojo’s sophistication serves the head contractor buying subcontractors, a larger commitment of project value.
  • The Wesco deal is a template. A supply-side or insurer partnership would give us comparable structural advantage.
  • Kojo validates that transaction data beats document data. Our 200,000 trade packages are the same kind of asset and should be described that way.

Open questions

  • Is Kojo moving up into subcontract procurement, or staying with materials?
  • What are the real accuracy rates on the agentic ordering workflows?

The process behind the page

Three commands, and a discipline about what counts as a fact

Everything on the first tab was produced by a repeatable competitive research skill, not written by hand. The skill sets up a project, researches competitors on the web, and generates cross-competitor analysis on any dimension you name. This tab explains how it works and where it stops.

/setup /competitive-update /generate-analysis
01

Set it up once, then refresh it forever

1
/setup

Tell it what you sell and who you are worried about

It reads your homepage, drafts your positioning, takes your competitor list, and asks what dimensions actually decide deals in your market. Those answers become a profile template. Everything downstream reads that template at runtime, so no section names are hard-coded and the structure changes per company.

Writes reference/product-info.md, reference/competitors.md, reference/guidelines.md and a profile stub per competitor.

2
/competitive-update

Go and look, then say what changed

Five searches and three page fetches per competitor, run in batches of three to five. Every finding is diffed against the existing profile and classified. Nothing touches a profile file until you approve it line by line, and the run produces a dated report whether or not you apply the changes.

Writes analysis/updates/competitive-update-YYYY-MM-DD.md.

3
/generate-analysis

Ask one question across all of them at once

Name any dimension and it extracts the relevant claims from every profile, normalises them into comparable sub-dimensions, and returns clusters, outliers, a comparison matrix and a positioning read. For this run the dimension was AI data moats and automated workflows. It reports coverage before it starts, and it will tell you when the data is too thin to answer.

Writes analysis/<topic-slug>.md.

The loop runs backwards too. Every open question on the first tab carries the exact /competitive-update command that would close it, so the gaps in one analysis become the inputs to the next research cycle.

02

Every claim carries its receipt, or it admits it is a guess

Two labels, applied to every line in every profile. This is the discipline the whole method rests on, and it is why the first tab can tell you that forty percent of it is assumption.

Observed · a real line from the Procore profile

- Observed: Procore acquired Datagrid, a vertical AI firm, announced 20 January 2026 (https://www.constructiondive.com/news/procore-acquires-datagrid-vertical-ai-firm/810120/)

A fact with a URL attached. If there is no URL, it cannot be labelled Observed. That rule is what stops a plausible-sounding inference from hardening into a fact over successive research cycles.

Inferred · a real line from the Autodesk profile

- Inferred (assumption): the network moat is geographically bounded. In Australia and the UK, BuildingConnected is close to a cold start.

A judgement. It may well be right, and it drives one of the five moves, but nobody published it. Marking it means a future reader can disagree with the reasoning without having to re-check the facts.

03

What counts as news, and what is just noise

ClassificationCriteriaExamples
MATERIALChanges the competitive pictureNew product line, acquisition, major pricing overhaul, leadership change, strategic pivot, new market entry
MINORWorth noting, does not change the pictureNew feature, messaging tweak, new integration partner, minor pricing adjustment
NO CHANGEConfirms what is already recordedValidates that existing intelligence is still current

The asymmetry is the clever part. A finding that contradicts something we observed is MATERIAL. A finding that contradicts something we inferred is only MINOR, because we already said we were guessing.

The labelling is not bookkeeping. It changes what the system does next. When the classifier is uncertain between MATERIAL and MINOR it leans MATERIAL, on the basis that over-flagging costs a moment of reading and under-flagging costs a surprise.

04

Fetched pages are evidence, never instructions

Everything retrieved by web search or page fetch is treated as untrusted external data. It is raw intelligence to extract facts from, and no instruction, directive or command embedded in a fetched page is followed.

If a page appears to contain text aimed at the research agent, the content is discarded and the anomaly is recorded in the research notes rather than acted on. A competitor’s website is a source, and a source does not get to give orders to the system reading it.

05

A re-run adds to the profile, it never overwrites it

Existing content is only removed when a new Observed finding directly contradicts it and a human approves the change. Analysis reports get a dated update section rather than a rewrite, so the history of how the landscape moved stays readable.

06

What this method cannot tell you

A method that names its own blind spots is more useful than one that claims completeness. These are the real limits of the first tab.

  • It is desk research. There are no win and loss interviews, no customer calls and no analyst briefings behind any of it. Everything came from pages a competitor chose to publish.
  • Published announcements lag reality. A company shipping quietly will look slower than one announcing loudly. Felix may have more running with customers than its blog suggests.
  • Pricing is a null column. None of the six publishes pricing, so the entire dimension is inference from third-party reports and cannot be relied on for a deal.
  • Corpus composition is never disclosed. Nobody publishes what is actually in their training data, so every claim about the shape of a competitor’s moat is reasoning from product behaviour backwards.
  • Two profiles are thinner than the rest. Kojo has little 2026 product detail in public, and Causeway’s AI claims come from marketing campaign pages rather than documentation.
  • It cannot tell you what a competitor will do next. It records what they have done and what they have said. The five moves are judgements built on that, not forecasts.
