# Felix Group Holdings (ASX:FLX)

*Category: Direct, Australian | Researched: 2026-08-08*

## 1. Snapshot

- Observed: Felix is headquartered in Brisbane and listed on the ASX, selling vendor management and procurement software to construction, mining, government, utility, infrastructure and property companies (https://www.felix.net/about)
- Observed: over 8,000 users manage more than 115,000 vendors on the platform (https://www.felix.net/)
- Observed: group contracted ARR of A$13.0m as at 30 June 2026, up 51% from A$8.6m, made up of Enterprise A$7.5m, Marketplace A$1.8m and Nexvia A$3.7m (https://kalkine.com.au/news/announcements/felix-group-lifts-contracted-arr-to-130m-in-q4-fy26-adds-495k-new-enterprise-arr)
- Observed: Q4 FY26 added 12 deals worth A$495k of new enterprise ARR, including a three-year Downer renewal (https://kalkinemedia.com/au/news/announcements/felix-group-reports-130-million-group-arr-and-signs-three-year-renewal-with-downer-in-q4-fy26-update)

## 2. AI data moat

**A large vendor compliance and performance record, not yet turned into a shipped AI advantage.**

- Observed: Felix positions its edge as connecting supply chain understanding (compliance, historic performance, ESG insights) to intelligent tendering (https://www.felix.net/felix-for-construction)
- Observed: Felix is accelerating AI within the Vendor Management and Sourcing modules, describing them as "the beating heart" of the platform (https://www.felix.net/blog/supercharging-the-felix-platform)
- Observed: the AI announcement makes no specific claim about proprietary datasets powering the capability, and describes the work as discovery, experiments and testing with customers, with no release dates (https://www.felix.net/blog/supercharging-the-felix-platform)
- Observed: an AI-driven Bid Insights feature is flagged for broader rollout, with vendor monetisation reporting changes starting Q1 FY27 (https://kalkine.com.au/news/announcements/felix-group-lifts-contracted-arr-to-130m-in-q4-fy26-adds-495k-new-enterprise-arr)
- Inferred (assumption): Felix's corpus is supply-side and compliance-shaped. It knows whether a vendor is qualified, insured and performing. It has much less of the priced commercial detail that ProcurePro holds, because Felix's centre of gravity is vendor onboarding rather than the award decision.

## 3. Automated workflows

- Observed: the stated AI targets are vendor assessment, which is time-intensive today, and bid analysis, which is slow (https://www.felix.net/blog/supercharging-the-felix-platform)
- Observed: Felix's framing is that it brings vendor management and sourcing together rather than treating bid analysis in isolation (https://www.felix.net/blog/supercharging-the-felix-platform)
- Inferred (assumption): ProcurePro is further along on shipped procurement AI. BidLevel is in market with a stated three-minute turnaround, while Felix's public position is an announced intent plus Bid Insights in rollout. The gap is in what has shipped, and public announcements are an imperfect proxy for what is actually running with customers.

## 4. Product overlap with ProcurePro

Covers well: Vendor Management, prequalification and compliance, arguably deeper than ProcurePro.
Covers partially: Tenders, Compare, via sourcing and Bid Insights.
Does not cover at depth: Procurement Schedule, Scope of Works Library, priced breakdown comparison, Contracts & eSignature as a commercial workflow.

- Inferred (assumption): Nexvia is the piece to watch. It is a contractor-facing platform being integrated into Felix in the background, with a combined go-to-market planned for FY27. That integration is the route by which Felix could become a genuinely direct competitor rather than an adjacent one.

## 5. Market position and geography

- **ANZ:** the credible local rival, same city, same buyers, strong government and infrastructure penetration.
- **UK and North America:** Felix markets globally but the revenue base is Australian. Not a meaningful obstacle to ProcurePro's expansion.
- Inferred (assumption): Felix's segment mix (mining, utilities, government) means head-on collision with ProcurePro is only partial. Felix sells to asset owners and capital-intensive operators as much as to head contractors.

## 6. Pricing & packaging

- Inferred (assumption): enterprise subscription, quote only, with a separate vendor-side Marketplace revenue line worth A$1.8m ARR.
- Observed: vendor monetisation reporting changes begin Q1 FY27 (https://kalkine.com.au/news/announcements/felix-group-lifts-contracted-arr-to-130m-in-q4-fy26-adds-495k-new-enterprise-arr)
- Inferred (assumption): charging vendors as well as buyers is a structural difference. It funds supply-side density the way Autodesk's free tier does, at smaller scale.

## 7. Differentiators vs ProcurePro

- Deeper vendor compliance, ESG and risk tooling.
- Reaches beyond construction into mining, utilities and government, which suits diversified buyers.
- Listed and audited, which some enterprise procurement functions prefer.

## 8. Risks for ProcurePro

1. **Nexvia plus Felix, combined for FY27.** Moderate likelihood, real consequence. That combination is the closest anyone in Australia comes to ProcurePro's shape.
2. **Vendor-side monetisation** could fund a supply network in Australia that ProcurePro does not have.
3. **Incumbency in government and infrastructure** blocks a segment ProcurePro might otherwise expand into.

## 9. Opportunities for ProcurePro

- Felix's AI work is still largely in discovery and rollout by its own description. There is a window to establish the AI procurement position in Australia before the FY27 go-to-market.
- Felix's spread across six industries means less depth in commercial building specifically.
- ProcurePro's proprietary priced dataset is a genuine answer to Felix's compliance data. Different axis, and the one that touches margin.

## 10. Open questions

- What exactly does Bid Insights do, and how does it compare to BidLevel on accuracy and speed?
- What is the Nexvia integration timeline and combined product shape for FY27?
- How much of Felix's A$13m ARR comes from commercial head contractors, the buyer ProcurePro actually competes for?
