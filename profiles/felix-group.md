# Felix Group Holdings (ASX:FLX)

*Category: Direct, Australian | Researched: 2026-10-10 (re-checked)*

## 1. Snapshot

- Observed: Felix's shares are listed on the ASX (code FLX) and its principal place of business is Level 13, 239 George Street, Brisbane (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf, FY26 Annual Report lodged 30 September 2026)
- Observed: Felix says it is used by "construction, mining, government, utility, public infrastructure and property companies" (https://www.felix.net/about)
- Observed: group contracted ARR was A$13.0m at 30 June 2026, up 51% from A$8.6m, made up of Enterprise A$7.5m (+9%), Marketplace A$1.8m (+3.3%) and Nexvia A$3.7m (+26%) (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf, ASX Q4 FY26 update, 31 July 2026)
- Observed: the A$8.6m comparison excludes Nexvia, which Felix acquired in October 2025, so most of the 51% is acquired ARR; Enterprise ARR grew 9% (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf)
- Observed: Q4 FY26 added 12 deals worth A$495k of new enterprise ARR (7 new customers, 5 expansions), headlined by a renewed and expanded three-year agreement with Downer (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf)
- Observed: enterprise net revenue retention was 93% at the end of Q4 FY26 (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf)
- Observed: at 30 June 2026 Felix had approximately 80 Enterprise customers, 385 paying Vendor Marketplace customers and 202 Nexvia customers, and about 73.3k vendors were active across the network in FY26 (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Observed: Felix recorded 132,393 vendors in the Vendor Marketplace in Q4 FY26, and enterprise users grew 25% to just over 10k (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf and https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Observed: FY26 revenue was A$11.5m (FY25 A$8.3m), adjusted EBITDA was a A$4.2m loss and the statutory loss after tax was A$6.0m (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Observed: market capitalisation was A$12.46m at a A$0.030 last price per the ASX header on 10 October 2026 (the last trading day was 9 October) (https://asx.api.markitdigital.com/asx-research/1.0/companies/FLX/header)
- Observed: a new CEO, Chris Atkin, took over on 1 April 2026 and a new CFO, Stephen Hunter, was appointed on 3 August 2026 (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7419343.pdf and https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7663007.pdf)

## 2. AI data moat

- Inferred (assumption): a large vendor compliance and activity record that Felix is only now starting to turn into shipped AI features.

- Observed: Felix's own FY26 report cites platform activity as the data base: about 73.3k vendors active, RFQs up 23% to more than 30k, evaluations up 39% to about 17k and compliance documents up 34% to more than 300k (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Observed: Felix's March 2026 blog says it is "accelerating AI capabilities within the Felix platform", starting with the Vendor Management and Sourcing modules, and that it has "started partnering with customers for discovery, experiments and testing" (https://www.felix.net/blog/supercharging-the-felix-platform)
- Observed: the blog gives no release dates and makes no specific claim about proprietary datasets powering the AI (https://www.felix.net/blog/supercharging-the-felix-platform; the post names none, read in full 2026-10-10)
- Observed: Felix's pitch is that other bid-analysis AI lacks "the crucial connection to vendor management", and that it is building intelligence that brings vendor management and sourcing together (https://www.felix.net/blog/supercharging-the-felix-platform)
- Observed: Felix's September 2026 investor presentation says "AI products in validation stage" and lists "Bid analysis & Bid Insights" and "Vendor assessment" under AI "NOW", with automated verification, smart matching and benchmarks as "FUTURE" (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7756017.pdf)
- Observed: the Q4 FY26 update says Bid Insights has "completed internal testing and is commencing real-world user validation with key customers", with broader rollout "throughout Q1 FY27" (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf)
- Observed: Felix raised A$5.54m in a placement at A$0.038 per share (announced 28 September 2026), plus an SPP of up to A$1.0m, with proceeds "to fund AI product development, activation of the Felix vendor network, platform development and working capital"; tranche two of about A$2.9m is "subject to shareholder approval at the Company's annual general meeting to be held in November 2026" (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7756018.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): Felix's corpus is supply-side and compliance-shaped. It knows whether a vendor is qualified, insured and performing. It holds less of the priced commercial detail that ProcurePro holds, because Felix's centre of gravity is vendor onboarding and compliance rather than the award decision. Premise checked: Felix's Sourcing module does include side-by-side financial comparison and recommendation for award (https://www.felix.net/sourcing), so Felix does capture some bid data; how much priced detail it holds is not published.

## 3. Automated workflows

- Observed: the stated AI targets are vendor assessment, described by Felix as time-intensive, and bid analysis (https://www.felix.net/blog/supercharging-the-felix-platform)
- Observed: Felix's FY27 priorities include "improving vendor discovery, categorisation and matching" and "applying AI to automate activities that are currently manual" (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Observed: Felix released a Vendor Invite Approval workflow in Q4 FY26, which routes vendor invitations through a configurable approval chain (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): ProcurePro is further along on shipped procurement AI. ProcurePro markets BidLevel as extracting quotes "in as fast as 3 minutes" (a vendor claim, see reference/product-info.md), while Felix's Bid Insights was in customer validation at 31 July 2026 with broader rollout planned for Q1 FY27. Public statements are an imperfect proxy for what runs with customers, and the Q1 FY27 report (due around the end of October 2026) will show whether Bid Insights has rolled out.

## 4. Product overlap with ProcurePro

- Observed: Felix sells Vendor Management ("risk-based prequalifications, vendor self-service compliance management portal"), a Procurement Schedule, Sourcing and Contracts modules (https://www.felix.net/)
- Observed: Felix Sourcing covers tenders, "side-by-side financial comparisons" and a "Recommendation for Award" process (https://www.felix.net/sourcing)
- Observed: Felix's Contracts module is "a robust repository, configurable contract summaries capturing key information, update history and formal variations" (https://www.felix.net/)
- Inferred (assumption): Felix is strongest on Vendor Management, prequalification and compliance, arguably deeper than ProcurePro.
- Inferred (assumption): Felix overlaps ProcurePro on more modules than the earlier draft assumed: it has its own Procurement Schedule, Sourcing with comparison and award recommendation, and Contracts. The earlier draft listed Procurement Schedule and Contracts as not covered, and that was wrong. What remains unestablished is depth: priced line-item breakdown extraction, a scope of works library and eSignature were not found on felix.net module pages (searched felix.net home, /sourcing and /contracts pages, 2026-10-10).
- Observed: Felix describes Nexvia as "a SaaS business providing project and business management software primarily to SME contractors", acquired in October 2025 (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Observed: Nexvia's integration to date was "initial focus on sales", with "deeper integration of the Felix and Nexvia platforms" to follow product-led growth work (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7318265.pdf, 23 December 2025)
- Observed: Felix lists Nexvia among "further workflow tools" on the platform, and its FY27 priorities include improving "the underlying performance and economics of Nexvia", with new Nexvia pricing plans launching in September 2026 (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7756017.pdf and https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): the earlier claim of a "combined go-to-market planned for FY27" is not supported by the filings read on 2026-10-10 (could not confirm; searched the FY26 annual report, Q4 FY26 update, September 2026 investor presentation and December 2025 update). Felix's stated FY27 plan treats Nexvia as a separate SME product being fixed for margin. Nexvia remains the piece to watch because deeper platform integration is stated intent, but it is not a dated combined launch.

## 5. Market position and geography

- Observed: Felix's Q4 FY26 new enterprise contracts included "Stanwell Corporation (new", "Birdon (new)", "Austral Construction (new)" and "Brightstar Resources (new, name subject to customer approval)" (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): the credible local rival, same city, same buyers in part, with strong government and infrastructure penetration. Premises: both are Brisbane-headquartered (Felix per the annual report above, ProcurePro per reference/product-info.md); Felix's Q4 customers (Observed above, plus Downer).
- Observed: Felix is concentrating Enterprise resources on Australia and New Zealand, and says "International opportunities will continue to be assessed selectively but are not a primary area of investment" (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Observed: in December 2025 Felix signed a three-year contract with the Middle Eastern division of a global contractor, citing earlier client acquisitions in Canada and South Africa (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7318265.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): the revenue base is Australian and Felix is now explicitly de-prioritising international expansion, so it is not a meaningful obstacle to ProcurePro's UK and North America expansion (checked: FY26 annual report segments are Contractor, Vendor and Nexvia with no geographic revenue split, and the report's focus statement above; the Australian revenue share itself is not disclosed).
- Observed: Felix's FY26 report says "Felix now supports 80 Enterprise customers, across asset-intensive industries including infrastructure, construction, mining and utilities." (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): Felix's segment mix (mining, utilities, government) means head-on collision with ProcurePro is only partial. Felix sells to asset owners and capital-intensive operators as much as to head contractors. Premise: the "asset-intensive industries" line below.

## 6. Pricing & packaging

- Observed: Felix's September 2026 presentation gives tiered pricing with estimated ACV ranges: Enterprise above A$130k (full suite), A$25k to A$100k (governance portal) and A$5k to A$25k (Marketplace-only pay as you go); Vendors above A$12k, A$6k to A$12k and A$1k to A$5k (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7756017.pdf). These are the company's estimates, not published price lists.
- Observed: Felix reports a separate Vendor Marketplace revenue line, A$1.78m in FY26, from 385 paying vendors (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Observed: vendor monetisation reporting changes begin in the Q1 FY27 Appendix 4C (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): Felix publishes no public price list (searched felix.net home, sourcing and contracts pages for pricing, 2026-10-10); enterprise is quote only.
- Observed: Felix's FY26 segment table shows Vendor operating revenue of A$1,783k and a gross margin of (3%), against Contractor A$6,956k at 73% and Nexvia A$2,773k at 61% (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): charging vendors as well as buyers is a structural difference. It may fund supply-side density the way Autodesk's free tier does, at smaller scale. Premise: the Vendor segment gross margin line above, so on FY26 numbers the vendor side does not yet fund anything.

- Inferred (assumption): reaches beyond construction into mining, utilities and government, which suits diversified buyers. Premise: see the industries line in section 1 and the Stanwell and Endurance Mining wins (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7660509.pdf).

## 8. Risks for ProcurePro

- Inferred (assumption): Nexvia plus Felix, if integrated more deeply in FY27, would be the closest anyone in Australia comes to ProcurePro's shape. Moderate likelihood, real consequence. The Nexvia premise is intent only (see section 4), and Felix's funding is thin (see below).
- Inferred (assumption): vendor-side monetisation could fund a supply network in Australia that ProcurePro does not have, but Felix's Marketplace ARR was flat to +3% year on year in FY26.
- Observed: Felix's September 2026 presentation says "Smaller Tier 1 infrastructure pipeline due to 75% market share." (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7756017.pdf; a company claim)
- Inferred (assumption): incumbency in government and infrastructure blocks a segment ProcurePro might otherwise expand into. Premise: the "75% market share" line above (a company claim).
- Observed: Felix's audited FY26 accounts record "a working capital deficiency of $4,010,000" and "a history of losses and operating outflows", with net operating cash outflow of A$6.2m, ahead of a A$5.54m placement (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7761351.pdf)
- Inferred (assumption): Felix's capacity to out-invest ProcurePro in AI is limited by this funding position, which cuts the other way on risk 1.

## 9. Opportunities for ProcurePro

- Inferred (assumption): Felix's AI work is still in validation by its own account (Bid Insights in customer validation as of 31 July 2026, broader rollout planned for Q1 FY27). There is a window to establish the AI procurement position in Australia, and it is shorter than the earlier draft implied because Felix has just raised money specifically for AI development.
- Inferred (assumption): Felix's spread across industries means less depth in commercial building specifically. Premise: Felix's September 2026 presentation shows its enterprise ARR split across Infra Projects, Infra Services, Mining, Real Estate, Resources Owner, Utility Owner and Government, with no commercial-construction-only line (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7756017.pdf).
- Inferred (assumption): ProcurePro's priced, scope-attached award dataset is a different axis from Felix's compliance data, and the one that touches margin. This depends on the ProcurePro dataset claim in reference/product-info.md.
- Observed: Felix describes vendor ID, compliance verification and marketplace matching as its AI direction ("Automated verification", "Smart matching", "Deeper insight - risk, reputation, performance & benchmarks") (https://hermes-cdn.investorhub.com/announcement-pdfs/asx/7756017.pdf), which is a different target from priced bid-level comparison.
