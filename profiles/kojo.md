*Category: Adjacent, United States | Researched: 2026-10-10 (re-checked)*

- Observed: Kojo's October 2025 release calls Maria Davidson "co-founder and CEO of Kojo" (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016530530/en/Kojo-Poised-to-Revolutionize-Construction-Materials-Management)
- Observed: by April 2026 Kojo's own post quotes Micah Rodman as "CEO at Kojo", so Davidson is no longer named as CEO there (https://www.usekojo.com/blog/kojo-and-access-coins-evo-partner-to-close-the-procurement-to-finance-gap)
- Observed: Kojo's own site lists a 2024 item, "ABC's 2024 AI Tech Report features Kojo COO Micah Rodman's vision for AI-assisted procurement", so Rodman was COO before he was quoted as CEO (https://www.usekojo.com/about)
- Inferred (assumption): Micah Rodman is a co-founder (an undated secondary profile says so; could not confirm on 2026-10-10, and Kojo's own site does not describe him as one)
- Observed: a US$39m Series C led by Battery Ventures was reported in September 2022 and brought total raised to US$84m (https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/14/construction-tech-kojo-materials-management-supply-chain/)
- Observed: Wesco International invested US$10m in an extension to the Series C, with Battery Ventures leading the round and 8VC, Schneider Electric, RXR, Suffolk Construction, Human Capital and BoxGroup participating (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016530530/en/Kojo-Poised-to-Revolutionize-Construction-Materials-Management)

- Observed: Kojo states that over 600 contractors in the US and Canada rely on it to purchase over US$5bn of materials annually (vendor claim, October 2025) (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016530530/en/Kojo-Poised-to-Revolutionize-Construction-Materials-Management)
- Observed: in April 2025 Kojo cited "over 500 contractors" using the platform, so the stated customer count rose by 100+ in about six months (vendor claims) (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250418536028/en/Kojo-Launches-AI-Tool-Suite-to-Help-Contractors-Combat-Rising-Material-Costs)
- Observed: Kojo was still operating independently under its own name as of its latest blog post, dated 28 April 2026 (https://www.usekojo.com/blog)
- Inferred (assumption): no acquisition, shutdown or new funding round has been announced since the Wesco extension (searched "Kojo acquired", "Series D", layoffs, Maria Davidson 2026 via WebSearch and the Kojo blog index, 2026-10-10)

- Inferred (assumption): **Why an adjacent player is in a top-five list:** Kojo buys materials for specialty subcontractors, where ProcurePro buys subcontractors for head contractors. Different buyer, same underlying bet. It is the clearest working example of a procurement data moat built from transaction flow, and it sets the expectation US buyers now bring to any procurement AI pitch.

## 2. AI data moat

- Inferred (assumption): **Live transaction flow plus a distributor's rails, which is a different and arguably harder-to-copy asset than document history.**

- Observed: the Kojo Intelligence Layer uses OpenAI GPT-4 and Hugging Face transformer models alongside statistical and machine learning methods to help contractors find project-appropriate materials at optimal prices (undated post) (https://www.usekojo.com/blog/kojo-intelligence-layer)
- Observed: Wesco and Kojo are co-developing digital commerce tools that blend Kojo's procurement platform with Wesco's national distribution network, and their first joint product is Project POs, aimed at long-lead, high-complexity purchase orders, particularly for data centre construction (https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/09/17/wesco-invests-kojo-ai-procurement-tools-construction/)
- Observed: Kojo's own site lists Wesco, Mayer, Rexel, Hajoca, Graybar and Border States as supplier integrations (https://www.usekojo.com/blog)
- Inferred (assumption): US$5bn a year of purchases is a live pricing signal, not an archive, if the vendor figure holds. It updates continuously and reflects what was really paid, which is a stronger basis for price intelligence than quoted or awarded figures alone.
- Inferred (assumption): taking investment from a distributor is the strategically interesting move. It converts a data moat into a supply relationship, which a software-only competitor cannot replicate by shipping features. ProcurePro has no equivalent supply-side alliance (per reference/product-info.md; see "For the lead" in the val log).

## 3. Automated workflows

- Observed: Kojo launched an AI tool suite in April 2025 that compares pricing across suppliers, forecasts material cost changes and availability, and automates purchasing recommendations (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250418536028/en/Kojo-Launches-AI-Tool-Suite-to-Help-Contractors-Combat-Rising-Material-Costs)
- Observed: Kojo's September 2025 post on Project POs says contractors can "Deploy AI agents to assist in all procurement activities, scheduling tasks, and distributor follow ups" (https://www.usekojo.com/blog/wesco-partners-with-kojo-to-solve-complex-purchase-orders-for-contractors)
- Observed: the same post says AI-powered insights "automate submittal reviews, material ordering, and release & delivery scheduling" (https://www.usekojo.com/blog/wesco-partners-with-kojo-to-solve-complex-purchase-orders-for-contractors)
- Observed: Kojo's Business Wire release carries the same agent and automation wording, announced alongside the Wesco investment in autumn 2025 (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016530530/en/Kojo-Poised-to-Revolutionize-Construction-Materials-Management)
- Observed: on 28-29 April 2026 Kojo announced an integration with Access Coins Evo, stating that purchase orders, invoices and receipts created in Kojo "automatically sync" to Access Coins Evo and that it is "available now" (https://www.usekojo.com/blog/kojo-and-access-coins-evo-partner-to-close-the-procurement-to-finance-gap)
- Inferred (assumption): the agent and ordering language is vendor wording; no independent evidence found of what the agents execute on their own versus recommend, and no agent-specific launch date beyond the September 2025 Wesco announcement (searched "Kojo AI agents launch 2026" via WebSearch, 2026-10-10)
- Inferred (assumption): if the agents do chase distributors and schedule deliveries as described, Kojo has crossed from "AI reads your documents" into "AI completes the transaction". Chasing a distributor for a delivery date is a task, not a summary. That is a rung above where BidLevel currently sits.

## 4. Product overlap with ProcurePro

- Inferred (assumption): direct functional overlap is small. The overlap that matters is conceptual: both claim price intelligence from proprietary procurement data, and both are selling automation of the buying cycle.
- Observed: Kojo's pricing page lists "Subcontracts" among its procurement features, so some subcontract purchasing is in scope (https://www.usekojo.com/pricing)
- Inferred (assumption): the risk is not that Kojo takes ProcurePro's customers. It is that Kojo, Procore and Autodesk together set a US market expectation of agentic procurement, and a tool that extracts and compares looks dated against it.

## 5. Market position and geography

- Observed: Kojo states its customers are in the US and Canada (vendor claim) (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016530530/en/Kojo-Poised-to-Revolutionize-Construction-Materials-Management)
- Observed: Kojo's April 2026 integration is with Access Coins Evo, an ERP from The Access Group (https://www.usekojo.com/blog/kojo-and-access-coins-evo-partner-to-close-the-procurement-to-finance-gap)
- Inferred (assumption): Kojo has no UK, ANZ or Middle East presence (searched Kojo UK, Australia, Canada office expansion via WebSearch, 2026-10-10; found only a San Francisco HQ and a US and Canada customer claim). The Access integration is a software link and does not show a local operation.
- Inferred (assumption): Kojo is a read on the US market ProcurePro is entering rather than a competitor it will meet in deals.

## 7. Differentiators vs ProcurePro

- Inferred (assumption): real-time price signal from actual purchases rather than awarded packages (rests on the vendor US$5bn figure above).
- Inferred (assumption): agents that execute, including third-party follow-up (rests on Kojo's own agent wording; execution not independently shown, could not confirm on 2026-10-10).
- Observed: Kojo's release says it received Series C extension funding from "Wesco International (NYSE: WCC), a leading provider of business-to-business distribution, logistics services and supply chain solutions" (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016530530/en/Kojo-Poised-to-Revolutionize-Construction-Materials-Management)
- Inferred (assumption): a supply chain partner with a balance sheet behind the product (premise: Wesco is NYSE-listed, Observed above).

## 8. Risks for ProcurePro

1. Inferred (assumption): **Expectation setting in the US.** Highest likelihood. US buyers will ask what ProcurePro's agents do, not what its AI reads.
2. Inferred (assumption): **Category adjacency creep.** Materials and subcontract procurement converge in the head contractor's total spend picture, and whoever gets there first frames the category. Kojo's pricing page already lists Subcontracts.
3. Inferred (assumption): **Distributor-backed economics** are a pricing model ProcurePro cannot currently match.

## 9. Opportunities for ProcurePro

- Inferred (assumption): Kojo serves the subcontractor buying materials. Nobody with Kojo's sophistication serves the head contractor buying subcontractors, which is a larger commitment of project value.
- Inferred (assumption): the Wesco deal is a template. A supply-side or insurer partnership would give ProcurePro a comparable structural advantage.
- Inferred (assumption): Kojo validates the thesis that transaction data beats document data. ProcurePro's trade-package corpus is the same kind of asset and should be talked about that way (figure per reference/product-info.md).
