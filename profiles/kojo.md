# Kojo

*Category: Adjacent, United States | Researched: 2026-08-08*

## 1. Snapshot

- Observed: Kojo is a materials procurement platform for construction contractors, based in San Francisco, founded 2018 by Maria Davidson, Ryan Gibson and Micah Rodman (https://tracxn.com/d/companies/kojo/__9bC3hRKmoIfOZkN55nm11_MbjNgp_w2hcO-XWDvr7-w)
- Observed: roughly US$94m raised across five rounds, including a US$10m Series C extension from Wesco International in October 2025 (https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/09/17/wesco-invests-kojo-ai-procurement-tools-construction/)
- Observed: more than 600 contractors across the US and Canada purchase over US$5bn of materials annually through the platform (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016530530/en/Kojo-Poised-to-Revolutionize-Construction-Materials-Management)

**Why an adjacent player is in a top-five list:** Kojo buys materials for specialty subcontractors, where ProcurePro buys subcontractors for head contractors. Different buyer, same underlying bet. It is the clearest working example of a procurement data moat built from transaction flow, and it sets the expectation US buyers now bring to any procurement AI pitch.

## 2. AI data moat

**Live transaction flow plus a distributor's rails, which is a different and arguably harder-to-copy asset than document history.**

- Observed: the Kojo Intelligence Layer uses OpenAI GPT-4 and Hugging Face transformer models alongside statistical and machine learning methods to help contractors find project-appropriate materials at optimal prices (https://www.usekojo.com/blog/kojo-intelligence-layer)
- Observed: the Wesco partnership combines Wesco's distribution network with Kojo's platform to give contractors real-time visibility from quote to jobsite delivery (https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/09/17/wesco-invests-kojo-ai-procurement-tools-construction/)
- Inferred (assumption): US$5bn a year of actual purchases is a live pricing signal, not an archive. It updates continuously and reflects what was really paid, which is a stronger basis for price intelligence than quoted or awarded figures alone.
- Inferred (assumption): taking investment from a distributor is the strategically interesting move. It converts a data moat into a supply relationship, which a software-only competitor cannot replicate by shipping features. ProcurePro has no equivalent supply-side alliance.

## 3. Automated workflows

- Observed: contractors can deploy AI agents to assist with procurement activities, scheduling tasks and distributor follow-ups (https://tracxn.com/d/companies/kojo/__9bC3hRKmoIfOZkN55nm11_MbjNgp_w2hcO-XWDvr7-w)
- Observed: the Wesco integration adds AI-driven automation of submittals, ordering and delivery scheduling, removing manual data entry and email-based tracking (https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/09/17/wesco-invests-kojo-ai-procurement-tools-construction/)
- Observed: an AI tool suite was launched specifically to help contractors handle rising material costs (https://aithority.com/machine-learning/kojo-launches-ai-tool-suite-to-help-contractors-combat-rising-material-costs/)
- Inferred (assumption): Kojo has crossed from "AI reads your documents" into "AI completes the transaction". Chasing a distributor for a delivery date is a task, not a summary. That is a rung above where BidLevel currently sits.

## 4. Product overlap with ProcurePro

Direct functional overlap is small. The overlap that matters is conceptual: both claim price intelligence from proprietary procurement data, and both are selling automation of the buying cycle.

- Inferred (assumption): the risk is not that Kojo takes ProcurePro's customers. It is that Kojo, Procore and Autodesk together set a US market expectation of agentic procurement, and a tool that extracts and compares looks dated against it.

## 5. Market position and geography

- **North America:** established with 600+ specialty contractors, reinforced by a distributor investor.
- **UK, ANZ, Middle East:** no meaningful presence found.
- Inferred (assumption): Kojo is a read on the US market ProcurePro is entering rather than a competitor it will meet in deals.

## 6. Pricing & packaging

- Inferred (assumption): subscription priced by contractor size or purchase volume, quote only. No public pricing found.
- Inferred (assumption): the distributor relationship opens the door to transaction-linked economics, which would let Kojo undercut pure subscription pricing over time.

## 7. Differentiators vs ProcurePro

- Real-time price signal from actual purchases rather than awarded packages.
- Agents that execute, including third-party follow-up.
- A supply chain partner with a balance sheet behind the product.

## 8. Risks for ProcurePro

1. **Expectation setting in the US.** Highest likelihood. US buyers will ask what ProcurePro's agents do, not what its AI reads.
2. **Category adjacency creep.** Materials and subcontract procurement converge in the head contractor's total spend picture, and whoever gets there first frames the category.
3. **Distributor-backed economics** are a pricing model ProcurePro cannot currently match.

## 9. Opportunities for ProcurePro

- Kojo serves the subcontractor buying materials. Nobody with Kojo's sophistication serves the head contractor buying subcontractors, which is a larger commitment of project value.
- The Wesco deal is a template. A supply-side or insurer partnership would give ProcurePro a comparable structural advantage.
- Kojo validates the thesis that transaction data beats document data. ProcurePro's 200,000 trade packages are the same kind of asset, and should be talked about that way.

## 10. Open questions

- Is Kojo moving up into subcontract procurement, or staying with materials?
- What proportion of Kojo's data comes through Wesco versus other distributors, and does that limit neutrality?
- What are the real accuracy rates on the agentic ordering and follow-up workflows?
