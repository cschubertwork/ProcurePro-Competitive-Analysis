# E1 (EstimateOne)

*Category: Direct, Australian (added in the 2026-10-10 re-check) | Researched: 2026-10-10*

## 1. Snapshot

- Observed: E1 describes itself as "Australia’s leading construction tender platform" (https://estimateone.com/)
- Observed: E1 "started its journey in 2009 in Melbourne, Victoria", founded by Michael Ashcroft and Andrew Ritchie, and "Self-funded from the start" (https://estimateone.com/about-us/)
- Observed: its careers page says "we’re a SaaS company headquartered in Richmond, Melbourne" (https://estimateone.com/careers/)
- Observed: the ABN register lists EstimateOne Pty Ltd as an Australian Private Company, "Active from 09 Sep 2009", ACN 130 242 554, with the business name "E1 CONSTRUCTION NETWORK" registered 13 May 2025 (https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View/24130242554, extracted 10 October 2026)
- Observed: E1 states "more than 100,000 Australian organisations used our platform to manage the construction tendering process" in the last year (https://estimateone.com/about-us/; the page does not name the year)
- Observed: E1 states "In 2025, Aussie Head Contractors posted 24,000 tenders at a total value of $354 billion" (https://estimateone.com/about-us/)
- Observed: E1's subcontractor page gives the same year as "24,049 tenders listed in 2025 alone" and "$354B worth tenders", with "900+ of the top builders in Australia" using E1 (https://estimateone.com/subcontractors/)
- Observed: E1's builder page gives different figures for the same year: "24,700 tenders listed in 2025 alone", "$364B worth of tenders" and "81,000+ subcontractor and supplier profiles on the network" (https://estimateone.com/commercial-builders/). The same page's Builder Business plan says "40,000+ enriched subbie profiles" and the homepage says "50,000+ subbies, 7,000+ suppliers" (https://estimateone.com/). E1's own pages disagree with each other; all are vendor claims.
- Observed: the careers page still carries an older, undated set of figures: "more than 94,000 organisations", "over 14,000 projects worth a total of $137 billion" (https://estimateone.com/careers/)
- Observed: in January 2022 Business News Australia reported E1 had "annual revenue of more than $10 million" and "more than 50,000 commercial builders, subcontractors and suppliers" (https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/big-hitters-bolster-estimateone-s-prospects-after-investing--35m.html, archive capture 2026, about 4.7 years old, so stale)
- Observed: E1 sells three sides of one network. Builders buy a tender-management subscription; "Tender listings are uploaded and managed by the builders themselves"; subcontractors and suppliers can sign up free (https://estimateone.com/tenders/)
- Inferred (assumption): headcount is not published. No employee count appears on the about-us or careers pages (searched both, 2026-10-10), and no primary source for it was found.
- Observed: E1 says "Our growth accelerated in 2022 after securing investment from Potentia Capital and Saniel Ventures." (https://estimateone.com/about-us/)
- Inferred (assumption): E1 is a founder-built platform that took growth capital in 2022 and is now compounding by acquisition (C-Link in the UK, 12Build in Europe); revenue today is not published, and the A$10m-plus figure is from 2022.

## 2. AI data moat

- Inferred (assumption): the moat is the supply-side network plus the documents that run through it: tender invitations, drawings and specifications, and the quotes subcontractors send back. It is broad and old (since 2009), with the top of the funnel in Australia. What it holds on awarded price and scope is not established.

- Observed: builders post the tenders themselves, including "Invitation to Tender (ITT) / Request for Quote (RFQ, Drawings and Specifications and Scope of Works (SOW)" (https://estimateone.com/tenders/)
- Observed: E1 says "We partner with all the major Aussie builders, and they post their tenders on E1." (https://estimateone.com/subcontractors/)
- Observed: quotes enter the platform in two ways: Quote Analysis says quotes "can be submitted directly by subcontractors and suppliers or uploaded by you and your team if received from an email" (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756366327-quote-analysis; see section 3 for the article)
- Observed: E1 shows subcontractors "awarded project information and estimator contact details added directly from tendering" (https://estimateone.com/subcontractors/)
- Observed: E1 tells suppliers it is "consolidating Australia’s construction tender data" and that they can "See exactly where your products, materials or brands are referenced in live tender and awarded project documentation" (https://estimateone.com/suppliers/)
- Observed: E1's builder support has a post-award collection ("Resources to guide you through platform features for projects in the Post-Award phase") and an article "Viewing Awarded Subbies in the Address Book" for "details and stats of awarded subbies" (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/collections/1705452961-procurement-phase-features)
- Observed: E1's supplier-facing page says suppliers can "look at years of historical data" (https://estimateone.com/what-is-estimateone-e1/)
- Observed: E1's Australian terms (last updated May 2026) let E1 use Customer Data to "develop, train, test and improve EstimateOne’s algorithms, models and AI Systems", and say "Anonymised data may be used more broadly" to "develop and commercialise our AI System with other customers" (https://estimateone.com/terms-of-use/)
- Observed: its UK and EU terms say the same in clause 7.2.6 ("EstimateOne may use Customer Data and User Inputs to develop, train, test and improve its algorithms, models and AI Systems"), limited to anonymised or aggregated data (https://estimateone.com/en-gb/terms-of-use-uk-and-eu/)
- Observed: the Quote Analysis FAQ says the opposite for that one feature: "your data is never used to train any large language models" and "The input data is not retained or used for training by E1 or any third party" (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756408600-quote-analysis-faq)
- Inferred (assumption): the two statements can both be true if the FAQ refers to third-party language models and the terms refer to E1's own models on anonymised data, but the FAQ does not say that. How the quote documents feed E1's own models is the unresolved point (see section 10).
- Observed: E1 bars outsiders from the same use: "Platform data can’t be used to build a competing product or to train AI/ML models without our written consent." (https://estimateone.com/terms-of-use/)
- Observed: the 12Build release says the two platforms will "share learnings, data and capabilities" (https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/23/3224867/0/en/e1-welcomes-12build-as-part-of-its-global-construction-procurement-platform.html, archive capture 2026)
- Inferred (assumption): ProcurePro's own stated corpus, as recorded in reference/product-info.md, is "more than 6,000 construction projects", "over $130 billion" and "more than 200,000 trade packages" (a vendor claim, checked in that file, not re-fetched here)
- Inferred (assumption): E1's corpus is wider at the top of the funnel and ProcurePro's is deeper at the bottom. E1 sees the invitation, the documents and every quote sent in response, including the losing ones, across 900+ builders. ProcurePro's stated corpus is the packages its customers actually awarded, with priced scope and exclusions. Whether E1 records the awarded price and final scope against each tender is not published; what E1 shows is "awarded project information" and award "stats" in an address book.
- Inferred (assumption): on volume E1 plausibly leads (24,000 tenders in a year against ProcurePro's 200,000 packages over six years is not like for like, because a tender holds several packages), so "ProcurePro holds the largest corpus" is not safe against E1; "ProcurePro holds awarded commercial terms" is the claim that still needs a counter-example.
- Inferred (assumption): E1's moat compounds through usage on both sides (builders bring tenders, subcontractors bring quotes) and is hard to approximate from public data, because tender documents are private to invited parties. The binding constraint is permission: customers decide what E1 may learn from, and the FAQ wording shows E1 is cautious on quote data specifically.

## 3. Automated workflows

- Observed: E1's Quote Analysis help article, dated 13 April 2026, says the tool "leverages AI to automatically read and compare two to five subcontractor or supplier quotes within a single trade package" (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756366327-quote-analysis)
- Observed: the same article in E1's international (UK) support site is dated 9 October 2025 (https://international.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756479680-quote-analysis)
- Inferred (assumption): the feature existed by October 2025; the April 2026 date is the Australian article's date and may be an update, not a launch.
- Observed: E1's resources page lists "Builder – AI-enabled quote analysis on E1", last updated 21 January 2026 (https://estimateone.com/resources/for-main-contractors/). Its co-founder writes "We’re at the very beginning of this journey" and "working hand in hand with a number of estimators over the past few months" (https://estimateone.com/for-main-contractors/ai-enabled-quote-analysis-coming-soon/)
- Observed: the report holds "Unadjusted Pricing" (a side-by-side comparison), "Suggested Clarifications" ("AI-generated questions for each subcontractor") and an "Inclusions & exclusions" scope comparison (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756366327-quote-analysis)
- Observed: with a trade-specific Bill of Quantities the tool "automatically maps line items from subcontractor quotes to your project’s Bill of Quantities", and E1 claims this will "reduce time spent structuring and reviewing quotes from days to minutes" (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756366327-quote-analysis; a vendor claim)
- Observed: limits: "2 to 5 quotes per analysis", per package ("You cannot analyse quotes from different packages together"), and Word and email files are unsupported (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756366327-quote-analysis)
- Observed: E1 frames it as "a decision-support tool, not a decision-making tool" and "Our goal is to augment estimators, not to automate their process" (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756366327-quote-analysis and https://estimateone.com/for-main-contractors/ai-enabled-quote-analysis-coming-soon/)
- Observed: results carry sources: "For PDF quotes, the quote viewer opens with sources highlighted in green." (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756366327-quote-analysis)
- Observed: Quote Analysis "may be automatically disabled for certain project types, such as Defence projects" (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756408600-quote-analysis-faq)
- Observed: E1's builder page adds a "Document Matrix" claim: "Quotes are consolidated into a clean side by side view, key scope or price discrepancies are flagged for review, and clarification lists are drafted to speed up responses." (https://estimateone.com/commercial-builders/)
- Observed: on the subcontractor side E1 says "E1 gives you instant clarity on project fit using AI powered tools" (https://estimateone.com/subcontractors/)
- Observed: in the UK, C-Link (E1's subsidiary, see section 5) sells "AI-Driven Tender Analysis" and claims "AI comparison reduces tender evaluation time from 12 hours to 4" (https://c-link.com/features/ai-driven-tender-analysis/; a vendor claim; the same page's headline "8x Time Saved" does not match 12 hours to 4, which is 3x)
- Observed: E1 claims customers "complete tenders as much as 75% faster than they would using traditional methods" (https://info.estimateone.com/e1-x-c-link-procurement-connected; a vendor claim)
- Inferred (assumption): the automation depth is extraction, comparison and drafted clarifications, with a human decision; no recommendation, award or agent that acts was found (searched E1's help article, FAQ, builder and subcontractor pages, 2026-10-10). That is the same rung as ProcurePro's comparison step and below the "agents that act" framing in the analysis.
- Inferred (assumption): no GA or beta status is stated; the Quote Analysis article and its FAQ carry no beta label (searched both, 2026-10-10).

## 4. Product overlap with ProcurePro

- Inferred (assumption): E1 is strongest on the front end of procurement (send-out, coverage, addenda, collecting quotes), which ProcurePro treats as the Tenders module, and now reaches into Compare in Australia. It is a tender-management platform first.
- Observed: Procurement schedule: E1 has a "Letting Schedule" for tracking the project and "Budget management" in the procurement phase (https://international.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1707121414-the-letting-schedule-video, dated 11 Oct 2024)
- Observed: Tenders: Builder Core includes "Automate project creation and addenda management" and unlimited public and private projects (https://estimateone.com/commercial-builders/)
- Observed: Compare: Quote Analysis reads and compares quotes within a package, as in section 3 (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/articles/1756366327-quote-analysis); its plan is covered in section 6
- Observed: Vendor management: Builder Business adds "Integrate with the Australian Business Register for real-time ABN updates" and syncing an address book with the E1 Network (https://estimateone.com/commercial-builders/)
- Observed: Data and analytics: the "Coverage Dashboard" sits in the Builder Business feature list (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/collections/1705371865-getting-started-on-e1)
- Observed: Contracts and eSignature in the UK: C-Link lists "e-Signatures" and a document creator ("create and edit contract documents, then issue to your network with just one click") (https://www.c-link.com/ and https://info.estimateone.com/e1-x-c-link-procurement-connected)
- Inferred (assumption): in Australia, E1 shows no contract creation or eSignature, no scope-of-works library, no Lessons Learnt and no priced-history library (searched estimateone.com builder, home, tender-management and UK pages for eSign, contract, scope of works, scope library, 2026-10-10). Together with the UK C-Link coverage, E1 now spans pre and post-award in the UK but only pre-award plus Compare in Australia.
- Observed: E1 integrates with Procore and SharePoint ("Guides to Procore and SharePoint integrations") (https://builder.support.estimateone.com/en/collections/1754309781-e1-integrations)
- Inferred (assumption): the overlap is real on Tenders and Compare, partial on Procurement Schedule and Vendor Management, and absent on Scope of Works Library, Lessons Learnt and AI Pricing Library in Australia. It is a close fit with ProcurePro's whole shape only in the UK, via C-Link.
- Observed: E1's subcontractor page offers the "open tender noticeboard" and says "Understand your quoting performance and win rates with each builder" (https://estimateone.com/subcontractors/)
- Observed: E1's supplier page says "E1 helps Suppliers track where their products, brands, and materials are (and aren’t) specified in tender documents" (https://estimateone.com/suppliers/)
- Inferred (assumption): what E1 does that ProcurePro does not is subcontractor and supplier-facing: a subcontractor noticeboard, supplier specification tracking and win-rate analytics for subbies (premises: the two Observed lines above).

## 8. Risks for ProcurePro

- Inferred (assumption): E1 puts AI quote comparison in front of the same head-contractor estimator in Australia, at a published price, with a free subcontractor network behind it. High likelihood, because it is live. It weakens any claim that ProcurePro is the only product doing AI comparison inside the tender in ANZ.
- Inferred (assumption): the UK pair (E1 for tender, C-Link for post-award with e-signatures and AI tender analysis) is a direct hit on ProcurePro's UK expansion, under a parent with a controlling private-equity owner and a track record of acquiring (C-Link in July 2025, 12Build in January 2026). Moderate likelihood; the integration depth is unproven.
- Inferred (assumption): if E1 starts recording awarded price and scope against its tender data at scale, it could approximate ProcurePro's awarded-packages corpus from a larger base. Low to moderate likelihood; nothing published shows it doing so yet, and its own customer-facing text says quote inputs are not used for training.
- Observed: the Companies House persons-with-significant-control register for C-Link shows E1's two founders, Michael Robert Ashcroft and Andrew Paul Ritchie, each "Ceased" on 12 January 2026, while Timothy John Reed and Andrew James Nathaniel Gray remain active at 25% to 50% (https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/09791821/persons-with-significant-control)
- Inferred (assumption): Reed and Gray are the Potentia Capital principals named in the 2022 deal reporting ("Potentia Capital, headed by Reed and former managing director of Archer Capital Andrew Gray", https://www.businessnewsaustralia.com/articles/big-hitters-bolster-estimateone-s-prospects-after-investing--35m.html), so the register is consistent with Potentia holding control by January 2026, 11 days before the 12Build announcement. This is a match on names across two sources, not a filing that says so.
- Inferred (assumption): a private-equity controlled E1 with acquisition appetite is more likely to buy into ProcurePro's niche (or a rival) than a founder-led one. Possible, no evidence of intent.

## 9. Opportunities for ProcurePro

- Inferred (assumption): E1's AI is a first pass for an estimator at tender time, with a human decision, five quotes at a time, one package at a time. ProcurePro's workflow runs on through recommendation, approval and contract in one product in Australia, where E1 stops (see section 4).
- Inferred (assumption): E1's decision-support stance ("not a decision-making tool") and the Defence-project exclusion show a cautious AI posture, which leaves room for the agentic and award framing ProcurePro can own.
- Inferred (assumption): E1's scale figures contradict each other across its own pages (24,000 against 24,049 against 24,700 tenders; 40,000+ against 50,000+ against 81,000+ profiles), so any buyer-facing "bigger network" claim from E1 can be tested.
- Inferred (assumption): E1's network is Australia-weighted; the UK is a team plus an acquired post-award product, not a large installed base. ProcurePro's UK customers include Bouygues UK, Purcell and Reds10 per reference/product-info.md, so a UK head-to-head is not one-sided in E1's favour.
- Inferred (assumption): ProcurePro can use the awarded-terms angle: E1's data is rich on who was invited and who quoted; ProcurePro's is on what was agreed. That distinction needs the ProcurePro dataset claim in reference/product-info.md to hold.
